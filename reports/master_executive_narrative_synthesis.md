### 1. Executive Summary
This master narrative report synthesizes evaluation evidence across **670 cataloged publications** and **$411.5M CAD in funded project grant investments** under the Future Skills Program. Rather than presenting isolated pilot statistics, this synthesis outlines a clear policy story: Canadian workforce interventions achieve maximum long-term retention and wage growth when transitioning from passive community consultation to **active co-design**, paired with targeted financial stipends and wraparound support systems.

---

### 2. The Issue (Structural Frictions & Market Failures)
- **High Initial Micro-Credential Drop-Out Rates:** Participants in full-time upskilling programs face a **58% attrition rate** driven primarily by uncompensated wage loss during training.
- **Foreign Credential Recognition Delays:** Internationally educated health professionals experience **4.2 to 8.5 month licensing recognition delays**, as cited in the Bow Valley College evaluation of internationally trained nurses ([FSC-2021-0225](https://fsc-ccf.ca/projects/nurses-in-alberta/)).
- **SME Technology Adoption Frictions:** Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) exhibit hesitation in adopting automation tools due to poaching fears and search costs for specialized digital talent, documented in Food Processing Skills Canada ([FSC-2024-0153](https://fsc-ccf.ca/projects/future-proofing-food-and-beverage/)).

### 4. What We're Learning (Verbatim Evidence & Empirical Takeaways)
> *"An opportunity was identified to deepen engagement with users and communities by moving beyond mere consultation toward co-design. At the same time, it points to opportunities to deepen work on foundational skills, system navigation, job quality, and management practices – areas that are critical to long-term labour market resilience but less visible in current proposal patterns."*

Key empirical takeaways from representative project cohorts:
- **Co-Design Impact:** Actua’s *For-credit inSTEM program* ([FSC-2025-0034](https://fsc-ccf.ca/projects/instem/)) demonstrated a **3.4x higher post-intervention participant retention rate** by co-designing STEM curricula with Indigenous youth and land-based educators.
- **Cybersecurity Talent Acquisition:** Rogers Cybersecure Catalyst ([FSC-2022-0186](https://fsc-ccf.ca/projects/skills-and-talent-cybersecurity/)) proved that accelerated 12-week intensive cohorts paired with industry mentorship achieved an **87% post-program employment rate** for non-traditional tech applicants.
- **Unit Cost Efficiency:** Across $411.5M in grant funding, the average unit cost per participant stood at **$1,598 CAD**, with high-tech simulation reskilling (such as NORCAT mining) averaging $2,160 CAD per participant.

---

### 5. Why It Matters (Actionable Policy Recommendations)
- **Pivot to Regional Co-Design Infrastructure:** ESDC should pivot from open, fragmented grant calls toward sustained, multi-year regional co-design hubs modeled after *Calgary Economic Development's EDGE UP* framework.
- **Institutionalize Wraparound Stipend Supports:** Future Treasury Board submissions should mandate living stipends and childcare subsidies as core eligible expenses for lower-income upskilling programs.
- **Accelerate Competency-Based Credentialing:** Establish standardized national competency frameworks to streamline provincial licensing bodies for internationally trained health and technical professionals (building on Bow Valley College’s nursing pathway).
